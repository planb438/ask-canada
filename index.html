The questions we get asked most, with the official page for each one.

✈️ Visiting Canada

  • Depending on your passport, you may need an eTA or a visitor visa to enter Canada.
  • Apply for an eTA only on the Government of Canada website. Other sites may charge extra.
  • Check what you need before you book your flight.
Official source: canada.ca →

🏠 Moving to Canada

  • There are several ways to immigrate, including economic, family and refugee programs.
  • Each program has its own rules and documents. Use the official tool to see which ones may fit you.
  • Processing times are published and updated by the government.
Official source: canada.ca →

🇨🇦 Citizenship

  • Permanent residents can apply for citizenship once they meet the requirements, including time lived in Canada.
  • Most adult applicants take a citizenship test and show they can speak English or French.
  • Use the official calculator to count your days.
Official source: canada.ca →

🎓 Studying in Canada

  • Most international students need a study permit and an acceptance letter from a designated school.
  • Check the rules about working while you study before you start a job.
  • Apply early. Processing can take longer at busy times of year.
Official source: canada.ca →

💼 Working & your SIN

  • Most people who aren't citizens or permanent residents need a work permit to work in Canada.
  • You need a Social Insurance Number (SIN) to work or get government benefits. Apply through Service Canada.
  • Keep your SIN private. Share it only when the law requires it, such as with your employer.
Official source: Service Canada →

🧾 Taxes & benefits

  • Canada's tax year is the calendar year. Most people file a return with the CRA each spring.
  • Filing can matter even with low or no income, because many benefits and credits are based on your return.
  • The CRA has a page just for newcomers.
Official source: CRA →

🩺 Health care

  • Each province and territory runs its own health insurance plan and issues its own health card.
  • Rules on who qualifies, and whether there is a waiting period, depend on where you live.
  • Consider private coverage until your provincial plan starts.
Official source: Health Canada →

🏦 Banking & money

  • You have the right to open a basic bank account, even without a job or money to deposit, if you show acceptable ID.
  • Compare account fees. Many banks offer newcomer packages.
  • Never send money to someone who says they're from the government and asks for gift cards or crypto.
Official source: FCAC →

🔑 Renting a home

  • Renting rules are set by each province, so your rights depend on where you live.
  • Get the lease in writing and keep a record of every payment.
  • Never pay a deposit for a place you haven't seen or verified.
Official source: CMHC →

🤝 Free newcomer services

  • Government-funded organizations across Canada help newcomers for free.
  • They can help with job searches, language classes, finding housing and filling in forms.
  • Use the official finder to see services near you.
Official source: IRCC →

🚗 Driving

  • Driver's licences are issued by each province and territory.
  • Whether you can exchange a licence from another country depends on the country and your province.
  • Some provinces have winter tire rules, such as Quebec.
Official source: Ontario.ca (example) →

⏱️ Processing times

  • IRCC publishes current processing times for most applications.
  • Times change often. Check them right before you apply and while you wait.
  • Don't book anything you can't cancel until your application is approved.
Official source: canada.ca →

FAQ

About Ask Canada

Is this a government website?

No. Ask Canada is an independent information site by Ask Authority. We explain things in plain language and link to official government sources. Always check the official page before you apply or pay.

Can you help me with my application?

We share general information, not legal or immigration advice. For advice on your own case, speak to a lawyer, a licensed immigration consultant (RCIC) or the government office directly.

How do I avoid immigration scams?

Use official websites, which end in canada.ca or gc.ca for federal services. The government will never ask you to pay with gift cards or cryptocurrency, and nobody can guarantee you a visa or job.

How often is this page updated?

We review it regularly, but rules can change at any time. The official links are always the most up-to-date source.
